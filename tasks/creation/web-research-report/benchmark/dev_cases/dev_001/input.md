# Research Request: Resolve and Assess an Open Scholarly-Data Project

Prepare a 1,700-2,200 word Markdown due-diligence report for the research analytics steering group of a mid-sized public university. The group is considering a two-year pilot of an open scholarly metadata source as the backbone of publication discovery and citation-network analysis.

The project name was omitted from the referral. Resolve it from this clue before assessing it:

> In 2021, a nonprofit previously known for a project measuring online research impact announced a successor to Microsoft Academic. The new project's name evokes the ancient library at Alexandria.

Research evidence current through **2026-06-30**. Do not assume the clue is accurate in every detail.

## Research Requirements

- Show the multi-hop identity chain: project, operating organization, the organization's former name, the relationship to Microsoft Academic, and the people or records that connect these facts. State any ambiguity.
- Assess the resolved project's data origins, coverage, work/author/institution identifiers, citation data, update cadence, API and bulk-data access, licensing, governance, and documented limits.
- Compare it with Crossref and OpenCitations for this university use case. Treat overlapping services and underlying data relationships explicitly; do not present dependent records as three independent confirmations.
- Examine at least one concrete current repository or release record and at least one full research paper or technical PDF that evaluates coverage, quality, bias, or data construction independently of the project's own marketing.
- Identify at least two material limitations or conflicts. Check whether later documentation, a changed version, scope difference, or source dependence resolves them.
- Recommend `pilot`, `pilot with gating conditions`, or `do not pilot`, with three acceptance tests and a fallback plan.

## Evidence Depth

Use at least **9 distinct inspected source bodies** from at least **5 publishers or repository owners**. Include at least three primary or official project/organization records, one repository or release artifact, one standards or upstream-data source, and two genuinely independent analyses, at least one of them a paper/PDF. Inspect relevant page or document bodies; search snippets do not count. Follow relevant links where needed and refine or paginate searches when the first result set is ambiguous or repetitive.

## Report Format

Lead with a one-paragraph identity resolution and recommendation. Include an identity/provenance table, a capability-and-limit comparison, a short conflict analysis, and an implementation decision section. Cite material claims with `[C#]` IDs and make the required `sources.json` and `evidence_graph.json` sufficient to audit the resolution chain, dependence relationships, and recommendation. Do not infer that broad coverage means complete or unbiased coverage, and do not claim legal advice about data licensing.
