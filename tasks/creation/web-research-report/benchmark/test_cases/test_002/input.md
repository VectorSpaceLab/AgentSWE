# Research Request: Provenance of the “First Computer Bug” Claim

Write a 1,600-2,100 word Markdown provenance report for a computing-history museum revising a permanent-gallery label. The existing draft says:

> On September 9, 1945, Grace Hopper found the world's first computer bug, a moth trapped in Harvard's Mark II computer.

Determine the earliest supportable primary-source account and recommend historically responsible label wording. Research records available through **2026-06-30**. Treat every element of the draft as a claim to test: date, person, machine, event, wording, and `first` status.

## Required Research

- Locate and inspect the digitized logbook record or best available institutional surrogate, preserving catalog identity, visible wording, date, physical-object context, and access limitations.
- Trace the claim backward from at least four later institutional, biographical, media, or educational tellings. Identify explicit citations, copied phrases, and likely shared origins; a cluster repeating one source is not independent corroboration.
- Resolve Mark II versus Mark III references, 1945 versus 1947 dates, who was present or credited, and the difference between `bug` as an existing engineering term and the taped moth as an `actual case`.
- Seek earlier primary evidence for use of `bug` in a technical sense and assess what it can and cannot establish about this incident.
- Build a dated provenance chain. For each conflict, explain whether it arises from catalog metadata, later recollection, paraphrase, transcription, or genuinely contrary primary evidence.
- Give a calibrated conclusion and propose a 45-70 word label plus a short catalog note. Preserve disputed attribution rather than choosing a famous name for narrative convenience.

## Evidence Depth

Inspect at least **10 distinct source bodies** from at least **6 publishers or collecting institutions**, including a primary artifact record or high-quality institutional surrogate, at least one historical patent/letter/book scan or comparable primary record about earlier terminology, at least two scholarly or curatorial analyses, and the later claim variants. At least two sources must be full PDFs or page-scanned primary records. Search snippets do not count.

## Report Format

Lead with `What the museum can support`. Include a claim-by-claim verdict table, provenance timeline, source-dependence map in prose, conflict analysis, proposed label, and research limitations. Cite claims with `[C#]`. Populate `source_relations` thoroughly enough to distinguish citation, copying, syndication, and a common underlying artifact. Link every quoted label fact to an exact passage or image/page locator.
