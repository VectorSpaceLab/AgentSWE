# Presentation Request: Carbon Dioxide Removal Landscape for a Research Funder

Create a 10-slide, 16:9 scientific landscape deck for a philanthropic research committee deciding how to distribute a new exploratory grant portfolio. Compare four carbon dioxide removal pathways: direct air capture with geological storage, bioenergy with carbon capture and storage, enhanced rock weathering, and biochar.

Use public evidence published on or before **2025-12-31**. This deck must clarify evidence quality and research gaps; it must not prescribe climate policy or claim that one pathway is universally best.

## Research and Source Rules

- Start with authoritative synthesis sources such as IPCC assessments, U.S. Department of Energy reports, and National Academies reports.
- Inspect primary peer-reviewed studies for pathway-specific mechanisms, measurement challenges, and reported ranges.
- Prefer review/meta-analysis evidence for cross-study ranges. Identify lifecycle boundaries, system boundaries, region, feedstock, storage horizon, and study type before comparing values.
- Do not combine cost, energy, removal potential, or durability estimates into a single chart when definitions or boundaries are materially incompatible. Use a native comparison table or separate small multiples and state why.
- Distinguish demonstrated removal, modeled potential, gross capture, and net durable removal.

## Required Analytical Story

1. Portfolio question and a one-sentence map of the four pathways.
2. Editable system diagram showing capture/removal, processing, storage pool, and reversal path for all four.
3. Evidence architecture: what can be compared, what cannot, and the shared vocabulary.
4. Direct air capture with geological storage: mechanism, maturity, constraints, and one study-grounded quantitative range.
5. Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage in the same evidence pattern.
6. Enhanced rock weathering in the same evidence pattern.
7. Biochar in the same evidence pattern.
8. Native editable comparison matrix across durability, measurability/MRV, resource demand, co-effects, maturity, and principal uncertainty. Include evidence-strength markers.
9. An analytical portfolio visual showing research-gap versus potential-decision value. Make it explicit when positions are committee-oriented synthesis rather than measured facts.
10. A balanced funding thesis: four grant themes, one for each pathway, plus cross-cutting measurement infrastructure and stop/learn criteria.

## Visual Evidence and Delivery

- Include at least two substantial mechanism graphics redrawn as editable shapes from cited scientific sources. If a source figure is reused as an image, verify and state its license/use basis and cite it directly.
- Use a white background, ink `#182026`, blue `#2F6B9A`, leaf green `#4D7C56`, magenta `#A33B6C`, and gold `#C69214`. Pair every color with a pathway label or symbol.
- Use a left-aligned 0.65-inch title band and a visible evidence-strength key on slides 4 through 9.
- Use native charts only for commensurable data; all comparison tables and diagrams must remain editable.
- Put scientific definitions, boundary notes, and range caveats in actual speaker notes on slides 2 through 9.
- Cite each material claim and visual on-slide with `[S#]`; map synthesis claims and calculation records in `source_manifest.json`.
- Add meaningful alt text and maintain at least 17 pt body text and 9 pt source text.
- Do not use a decorative globe, smokestack stock photography, a single composite score, or unlabeled maturity/cost bubbles.

The deck, source manifest, and run report must satisfy the global delivery contract.
