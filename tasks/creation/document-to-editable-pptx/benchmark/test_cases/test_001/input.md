# Presentation Request: Hugging Face Daily Papers Briefing for 2025-05-29

Create a concise 7-slide, 16:9 illustrated briefing for an applied-AI research team. The task is to discover and explain the **top three Hugging Face Daily Papers for the historical date 2025-05-29**.

## Research Requirement

Start from the dated Daily Papers page at:

`https://huggingface.co/papers/date/2025-05-29`

Determine the top three papers as ordered for that dated daily list. Confirm the order and the engagement evidence from inspected page bodies. If the dated page is blocked, incomplete, or JavaScript-dependent, do not rely on one search snippet: recover through at least two redundant public evidence paths, such as a dated public snapshot/feed, paper pages, author/project records that state daily placement, or another independently retrievable dated listing. Record which evidence establishes order, which establishes engagement, its access depth, and the access time. Do not treat current cumulative upvotes as though they were necessarily the historical vote count.

Inspect the full Hugging Face paper page and the paper/abstract or project page for each selected paper. Use primary paper evidence for contribution and reported results. Verify prominent numerical claims in the paper body or tables, not an LLM-generated summary.

Obtain a relevant lawful visual from each paper or project when its reuse basis is clear, with attribution. When it is not clear, create a faithful explanatory graphic from editable shapes instead. Do not use paper screenshots as a substitute for reading or rasterize tables into the deck.

## Required Story

1. Date, research question, and a one-sentence synthesis of what connected the day's leading work.
2. Ranked top-three overview with observed engagement and evidence-status labels.
3. Paper #1: problem, approach, one verified result, and one substantial visual.
4. Paper #2 in the same evidence pattern.
5. Paper #3 in the same evidence pattern.
6. Editable analytical comparison across a defensible common set of dimensions; use a native table and at least one native chart only if the source metrics are genuinely comparable.
7. Implications for the applied-AI team, limitations of the comparison, and three follow-up reading questions.

## Citation, Editability, and Design

- Use compact `[S#]` citations on every factual slide and full claim-level provenance in `source_manifest.json`.
- Every rank, engagement value, paper claim, result, and visual must resolve to inspected evidence. Mark partial or redundant verification honestly.
- Build ranking, comparison, and any quantitative data as native editable PowerPoint objects. Explanatory graphics must use editable shapes and connectors.
- Add speaker notes on slides 2 through 6 with methodological caveats and a 30-45 second talk track per slide.
- Add meaningful alt text to all paper visuals, charts, tables, and diagrams.
- Use a white background, near-black text, Hugging Face yellow `#FFD21E` as a limited accent, cobalt `#2457D6`, and raspberry `#C83E73`. Do not use a yellow wash or reproduce the Hugging Face logo.
- Use a fixed left title rail of 1.45 inches on content slides, a 0.5-inch outer margin, and a 10 pt minimum for source lines. Body text must be at least 17 pt.
- Avoid decorative AI imagery, generic brain/circuit icons, abstract claims about "breakthroughs," and unsupported cross-paper winner labels.

The deck, source manifest, and run report must satisfy the global delivery contract.
