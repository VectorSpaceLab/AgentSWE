# Fleet Charging Pilot: Operations Memo

## Status

The depot pilot served 48 battery-electric fleet vehicles during 2025. Demand rose as dispatchers shifted more overnight charging into the managed schedule. Operations recommends a controlled expansion, not a citywide rollout.

The weekly operations dashboard calls the system **98.4% available** for the year. That dashboard excludes scheduled maintenance windows and counts only hours when vehicles were booked to charge. The formal measurement rule supplied with this review uses a different denominator.

## Constraints Observed

1. Connector reset events remain the largest source of unavailable hours. A firmware update reduced their frequency after July, but the pilot does not establish how the change will behave at twice the connector count.
2. The 05:30-07:00 dispatch peak produces queueing when late-return vehicles miss their assigned overnight window. Dispatch sequencing and charger availability are linked, but the pilot did not isolate their independent effects.
3. Support tickets fell through the year. Ticket closure time was not consistently recorded, so the source does not support a time-to-resolution claim.

## Proposed Controlled Expansion

- **Infrastructure Services**: add 12 connectors at the existing depot by 2026-03-31; target formal monthly uptime of at least 97.0%; stop additional commissioning if any month is below 94.0%.
- **Fleet Operations**: introduce a late-return queue protocol by 2026-02-15; target successful-session rate of at least 96.0% for three consecutive months.
- **Finance & Performance**: review cost per successful session monthly; expansion checkpoint on 2026-06-30; do not set a savings target until labor allocation is measured consistently.

The requested approval is a six-month controlled expansion capped at 12 additional connectors, subject to the stated stop conditions and a June checkpoint.
