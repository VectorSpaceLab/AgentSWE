# Presentation Request: What DART Proved, and What It Did Not

Create a 7-slide, 16:9 illustrated research briefing for a university public-science forum. The audience is scientifically curious but does not work in orbital mechanics. The talk should explain the evidence from NASA's Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) without turning it into a mission celebration or a promise of complete planetary protection.

## Research Scope

Live-web research is required. Begin with the NASA DART mission and result pages, then inspect relevant linked NASA/JPL or peer-reviewed sources needed to verify the impact, the measured orbital-period change, the ejecta contribution, and the limits of generalization. Prefer NASA mission records and peer-reviewed papers over news coverage. Search snippets are discovery leads, not sufficient evidence for technical claims.

Use public NASA imagery only when its page provides a lawful-use basis and the image materially helps. Cite the image on-slide. If reuse status or retrieval is unclear, create a faithful editable explanatory graphic instead. Record access depth and any retrieval limitations in `source_manifest.json`.

## Required Content

1. Open with the literal question: **Can a spacecraft deliberately change an asteroid's motion?**
2. Show the Didymos-Dimorphos system and the test geometry with an editable, not-to-scale diagram.
3. Explain how the before/after orbital period was measured.
4. Show the period change as an editable quantitative visual and independently recompute the change in minutes from cited source values.
5. Explain momentum enhancement from ejecta without implying that the technique works identically for every asteroid.
6. Separate **demonstrated**, **inferred**, and **still unknown** in a native editable table.
7. Close with three implications for planetary-defense planning and two questions for the audience.

## Communication and Accessibility

- Use a near-black `#101820` background, white text, cyan `#35C2D8`, yellow `#FFD447`, and coral `#FF6B5E`; color may not be the only distinction.
- Use one large mission image or one substantial explanatory graphic on at least four slides. Avoid a gallery of small thumbnails.
- Add concise `[S#]` citations on every factual slide and full provenance in the manifest.
- Put speaker notes on slides 2 through 6. Notes should define terms, state caveats, and give delivery guidance; do not repeat visible copy.
- Give meaningful alt text to every non-decorative image, chart, table, and diagram.
- Use a minimum 20 pt body size and 10 pt citation size.
- Do not use a generic agenda, decorative space imagery, unsupported impact probabilities, or an invented asteroid-deflection scenario.

The deck, source manifest, and run report must satisfy the global delivery contract.
